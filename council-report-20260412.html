LLM Council Report

March Madness Pool Optimizer — 2027 Strategy Improvements · April 12, 2026

Question Posed

Given 15 years of retrospective bracket scoring data and a winner-take-all N=31 pool, what are the most impactful improvements to make before March 2027?

Critical Actions
  1. Fix bracket ranking: replace EV with P(1st) using Monte Carlo simulation against a modeled opponent field. Source the opponent model from ESPN public pick percentages or equivalent aggregate data. Gate: the system, re-run on 2026 data, ranks the det_champ_first bracket (or a bracket with similar structural properties) in the top 3.
  2. Fix F4 construction: enforce historical base rates on one-seed Final Four representation (cap at 2, expected value ~1.5) and weight champion selection toward the scoring function's highest-leverage round. Gate: across all generated brackets, average one-seed F4 count is between 1 and 2, and champion pick shows meaningful variation across bracket archetypes.
  3. Validate the opponent model against real pool results. Compare simulated opponent score distributions to actual pool score distributions from any available historical pool data. Gate: simulated max-of-30 score distribution has mean and variance within 15% of observed, or you have identified and documented the discrepancy and its directional effect on strategy.

Chairman's Analysis

Where the Council Agrees

Every advisor independently reached the same core diagnosis: the system is optimizing the wrong objective function. The pipeline maximizes expected bracket score (EV), but in a winner-take-all pool, EV-optimal brackets are losers. The 2026 result is the proof — the system generated the winning bracket and then buried it because its ranking function penalizes the variance that winner-take-all demands.

Four of five advisors agree the ML ensemble should be frozen or killed. BSS near zero means the ensemble is not beating seed-line baselines.

All five agree the Final Four construction is broken. Picking four one-seeds produces a consensus bracket. Historically ~1.5 one-seeds make the Final Four.

Where the Council Clashes

Can you learn anything from 14 years of backtesting?

The Contrarian and Statistician say no — 9-16% power means the signal-to-noise ratio is hopeless. The Executor and First Principles Thinker implicitly say yes.

Chairman sides with the Executor/First Principles: you cannot do fine-grained parameter tuning on 14 points, but you can validate directional changes. If the new ranking function would have surfaced the 2026 winner as a top-3 recommendation, that is a binary test with adequate power even at N=14.

Is the ML model worth keeping? Chairman sides with freeze, not kill. The model is not hurting if not being tuned. Removing it means losing infrastructure to detect if it ever climbs above the floor.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

The opponent model is unexamined. P(1st) is only as good as the simulated opponent pool. If the opponent model assumes chalk-heavy brackets, the system over-indexes on contrarian picks.

The scoring function structure was ignored. If championship points dominate, champion pick matters more than F4.

Calibration-to-pool interaction: deliberate miscalibration toward upsets could be a feature for winner-take-all.

The Chairman's Take

The council nailed the diagnosis but was sloppy on the prescription. "Optimize P(1st)" requires an opponent model, which is itself unvalidated. Risk of replacing one unvalidated optimization with another.

The 2026 retrospective is more diagnostic than acknowledged: examine which specific picks the EV ranker penalized in the det_champ_first bracket to design a non-statistical ranking fix.

The real unlock: separate game probability estimation from bracket construction strategy. Mediocre probabilities can still win pools if construction correctly solves the game-theory problem.

Advisor Responses

The Contrarian Advisor A

Fatal flaw: 14 data points at 9-16% power means drawing conclusions from noise.

What's broken:

1. The ranking function optimizes EV, not win probability. In a winner-take-all pool, this is the wrong objective entirely.

2. F4 construction is broken — picking all one-seeds produces a consensus bracket indistinguishable from the field.

3. Do not improve the ML model. BSS near zero means it is not contributing signal.

4. N=14 will mislead you. You need theoretical analysis, not more backtesting on insufficient data.

Stop tuning the engine. The steering wheel is broken.
The First Principles Thinker Advisor B

Wrong objective function. This is a game theory problem, not a forecasting problem.

1. Model the field and maximize P(winning | field distribution).

2. Stop picking four one-seeds for the Final Four.

3. Kill the ML ensemble — it is adding complexity without adding value.

4. This is a portfolio problem. You need a 3.2% win rate (1/31) to break even.

How to pick differently from 30 people and be right when they're wrong.
The Statistician Advisor C

You cannot learn from 14 data points. BSS near zero means you are optimizing noise. The 2026 result is survivorship bias — generating the winning bracket among many candidates is expected occasionally.

The opponent methodology needs scrutiny before any ranking change is meaningful.

1. Fix F4 calibration to match historical base rates.

2. Abandon ML until BSS is meaningfully above zero.

3. Accept the game theory framing — this is a contest against other humans, not against outcomes.

The Outsider Advisor D

You built a prediction engine when you needed a gambling strategy. Second place pays nothing. You need maximum variance, not maximum accuracy.

1. Model the pool — understand what your opponents will pick.

2. Stop picking four one-seeds for the Final Four.

3. Generate multiple bracket archetypes or select the single highest-ceiling bracket.

The 2026 result proves the concept — the system had the winning bracket and buried it under a ranking function that rewards mediocrity.

The Executor Advisor E

Monday-morning plan, one week total:

1. Fix 2026 retrospective data and scoring (half-day).

2. Force non-one-seeds into the Final Four via historical base rate constraints (one day).

3. Change bracket ranking from EV to P(1st) with Monte Carlo opponent simulation (2-3 days).

4. Stop touching the ML model. Freeze it and move on.
