Opponent Model Validation Report
=================================
Date: 2026-04-12
Pool: N=31 (30 opponents + 1 model bracket)
Trials: 500 pools per year × 14 historical years (2011-2025, excl. 2020)
Scoring: ESPN standard (R64=10 → CHAMP=320, perfect=1920)

Method
------
For each historical year:
  1. Load actual tournament outcomes
  2. Sample 30 opponent brackets from ESPN public pick distributions
     (seed-based fallback for 2012 which lacks ESPN data)
  3. Score all opponents against actual results
  4. Repeat 500 times for stable statistics

Results Summary
---------------
Metric              Mean    Range (across years)
Avg pool median:    1091    1008-1215
Avg pool winner:    1513    1441-1660
Avg score std:       217     193-243
Zero-score frac:   0.000    (no degenerate brackets)

Key Findings
------------
1. POOL WINNER SCORES ARE PLAUSIBLE: avg max-of-30 = 1513 (79% of perfect).
   This aligns with real-world ESPN pool data where winners of ~30-person
   pools typically score 1300-1600+.

2. SCORE SPREAD IS REASONABLE: avg std ~217 pts. This means ~68% of
   opponents fall within ±217 of the median (~875-1308). Enough variance
   to reward differentiated brackets.

3. NO DEGENERATE BRACKETS: zero-score fraction = 0.0000 across all years.
   The opponent model never generates pathological brackets.

4. CHALKY YEAR SENSITIVITY: 2024 (avg max 1607) and 2025 (avg max 1660)
   produce higher pool winner scores, consistent with more chalk-favorable
   actual outcomes. The model correctly tracks year-to-year difficulty.

5. ESPN vs SEED-BASED: 2012 uses seed-based pick rates (no ESPN data).
   Its distribution (median=1064, max=1506) is consistent with ESPN-years,
   suggesting the seed-rate fallback is adequate.

Discrepancies & Limitations
----------------------------
1. INDEPENDENT-PER-GAME ASSUMPTION: The opponent model samples each game
   independently from pick distributions. Real brackets have path consistency
   (if you pick Duke to lose in R32, you can't pick them in S16). Our model
   does enforce path consistency within generate_opponent_brackets(), but
   the pick_distribution values may implicitly assume independence.

2. NO CORRELATION STRUCTURE: Real pools have correlated brackets (everyone
   watches the same ESPN shows). Our model treats each opponent as
   independent. This may underestimate the "contrarian edge" — in reality,
   beating 30 correlated chalk-heavy opponents is easier than beating 30
   independent opponents with the same marginal pick rates.

3. MAX-OF-30 IS AN ORDER STATISTIC: The pool winner score is the maximum
   of 30 random draws. Small changes in the tail of the score distribution
   can meaningfully shift the max. Our 500-trial average stabilizes this
   but single-year estimates may vary ±30 pts.

Verdict
-------
The opponent model produces plausible score distributions. Pool winner
scores (avg 1513) and medians (avg 1091) are consistent with real-world
ESPN pool data. The model is suitable for P(1st) estimation and bracket
ranking in winner-take-all pools.
