Blind Spots Caught in Peer Review

Temporal validity — flagged by all 5 peer reviewers, missed by all 5 advisors. 19 years is not a homogeneous dataset. ESPN methodology changed. Tournament expanded 64→68 in 2011. Smartphone adoption, analytics culture, and COVID shifted picking behavior. Treating 2008 data as equivalent to 2024 data assumes stationarity that doesn't hold. Need per-year match rates and potential down-weighting of early years.
Coverage drift — if 2008-2012 data matches only 30% of team IDs, those years are effectively SEED_PICK_RATES anyway. The match rate by year must be a diagnostic output before any backtest runs.

The Recommendation

Fix the team-name matching layer first. Build a diagnostic that joins picks file names against codebase team IDs across all 19 years, computing per-year match rates. Exclude years below 80% match rate. Then check public_picks.json against yearly files for provenance. Then run the backtest with ESPN weight at 1.0 and 0.6, compare metrics, and let data choose the blend weight.

The data is not "valid enough to run." It is valid enough to be worth fixing properly before running. A backtest with silent fallbacks gives false confidence, which is worse than no backtest.

The One Thing to Do First

Write a diagnostic script that joins every team name in the picks files against the codebase team IDs, prints unmatched names with their year and file of origin, and outputs a per-year match rate. That single output tells you whether you have a minor cleanup task or a structural data problem.
