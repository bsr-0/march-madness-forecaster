LLM Council Verdict

5 advisors · 5 peer reviews · 1 synthesis — March 31, 2026

What are this repo's most critical limitations?
(march-madness-forecaster — 124K lines, 294 source files, 20+ scrapers, 6 model types, 50K MC simulations)

Where the Council Agrees

Three structural failures were independently confirmed across all advisors and peer reviews:

  • Scraper dependency is a single point of failure. All five advisors named it. 20+ scrapers tied to specific website structures means any TOS change, HTML restructure, or IP block silently corrupts the data pipeline with no robust detection mechanism.
  • The 3500-line CLI is a real architectural failure. Confirmed at 3,565 lines. Debugging under time pressure during Selection Sunday — the only window that matters — is nearly impossible.
  • The validation regime cannot support the architecture's complexity. LOYO folds contain ~63 games each (SE ~0.009 on Brier). The system's own documentation confirms no improvement smaller than ~1% Brier is statistically distinguishable from noise. The entire model selection process rests on a protocol that cannot reject the null hypothesis.

Where the Council Clashes

Forecasting vs. game theory: First Principles argues the system solves the wrong problem (should model competitors' picks, not accuracy). Peer review rebuts: pool_optimizer.py, leverage.py, and bracket_portfolio.py already model contrarian value. The critique is partly factually wrong — but the weighting question remains open.

Over-engineered vs. appropriately ambitious: The Contrarian treats 124K lines as evidence of failure. The Expansionist treats the architecture as an underutilized asset. Neither fully addresses: given ~63 games per validation fold, what complexity is defensible?

Blind Spots Caught in Peer Review

  • Confirmed data leakage: AUDIT_DATA_LEAKAGE.md documents an in-sample calibration path (p_fit = p_eval = p_arr). Every calibration metric may be optimistic.
  • Accumulated selection bias: 8 years of retrospective model selection inflates apparent performance beyond what the validation protocol can detect.
  • 2025 holdout consumed: Current year is 2026. No validated out-of-sample performance remains.
  • Temporal leakage risk: Unverified whether features use data not available at bracket lock time.

The Recommendation

Stop adding models and start measuring what you have honestly.

The fundamental problem is not the scrapers, not the CLI length, not the disabled GNN. It is that the evaluation infrastructure cannot tell you whether the system works. The LOYO validation is underpowered, confirmed data leakage corrupts calibration, and the holdout year has been consumed.

Fix the data leakage, re-run calibration cleanly, and accept that you likely cannot distinguish model quality at this sample size. Then either acquire more data (earlier years, regular season games) to increase validation power, or simplify the architecture to match what the data can actually support.

Everything else — scraper fragility, CLI complexity, disabled neural architectures — are real problems, but downstream of the core issue: you cannot currently measure whether your changes make the system better or worse.

The One Thing to Do First

Fix the data leakage in AUDIT_DATA_LEAKAGE.md — specifically the p_fit = p_eval = p_arr calibration path — refit calibration from scratch, and re-run your validation suite before touching anything else. Every other decision about model selection, architecture, and complexity depends on having calibration metrics you can trust.

The Contrarian — "A brittle house of cards dressed up as engineering"

The fatal flaw is that this system is a brittle house of cards dressed up as engineering. 20+ scrapers are a liability, not a feature — one website change kills the pipeline. The 3500-line CLI is disqualifying and signals failed abstraction. Training on 2016-2024 with 2025 as a single holdout year proves nothing due to March Madness variance. Production disabling GNN/transformer is a confession it doesn't work. 50K MC simulations mask garbage inputs producing confident garbage outputs.

The deepest flaw: one shot per year means you can't know if it works versus got lucky. 124K lines might be elaborate overfitting to historical tournament noise. The question is not "what does this system do?" but "what would prove this system doesn't work?" If you can't answer that, you don't have a forecasting system. You have a belief engine.

The First Principles Thinker — "You're solving the wrong problem"

The system inverted the problem. Bracket optimization for ESPN pools is a social game theory problem, not a forecasting accuracy problem. The optimal bracket is not the most accurate — it's the one that maximizes expected rank given what others will pick. The system built 124K lines of forecasting when the bottleneck is modeling the pick distribution of competition.

Historical data from 2016-2024 reflects rosters and coaches that no longer exist. The 50K MC assumes well-calibrated priors that can't exist with ~500 tournament games per training year. The actual limiting factor is solving for forecast accuracy when it should solve for contrarian value relative to pool behavior.

Peer review note: This critique was partially rebutted — pool_optimizer.py, leverage.py, and bracket_portfolio.py already model contrarian value and pool competition.

The Expansionist — "The use case has been artificially capped"

The system should be a prediction market edge finder, not just a bracket optimizer. Calibrated probabilities from 6+ model ensembles create divergence signals vs market-implied probabilities — should feed Kelly-criterion betting. The 8-year training window is an asset. Disabled GNN/transformer should be re-enabled selectively for upset detection. The architecture generalizes to NFL/NBA/NCAAW.

Peer review note: This was the weakest response. Multiple reviewers noted the system already implements Kelly criterion (leverage.py), pool optimization, and women's pipeline. The Expansionist recommended features that already exist.

The Outsider — "Large is not the same as reliable"

The 3500-line CLI is a fire alarm nobody's treating as a fire. "Pipeline -> runner -> predictor -> engine" is four words for the same thing — layers added when things broke instead of fixing design. 20+ scrapers mean the system can be destroyed by a TOS change.

The most honest limitation: the system cannot explain why it made a pick. You have six model types, Monte Carlo on top, bracket optimization on top of that. If the bracket is wrong, there's no audit trail a human can follow. Training on ~600 total games with complex neural architectures, validating on one tournament. Large is not the same as reliable.

The Executor — "Instrument the scrapers before anything else"

The bottlenecks that will actually bite: 3500-line CLI is a deployment disaster for Selection Sunday debugging. 20+ scrapers fail silently and randomly. Production disabling GNN/transformer means calibration may not account for the actual production model mix. 2025 holdout with 2026 current year means no validated out-of-sample performance. Long cold-start prevents re-running after late injury news.

Most actionable: instrument every scraper with timestamped health checks that alert on failure before they silently return stale or empty data.

Peer Review Highlights

Strongest responses: The Contrarian (A) was voted strongest by 3 of 5 reviewers. The First Principles Thinker (B) was voted strongest by 2 of 5.

Biggest blind spot: The Expansionist (C) was flagged by 4 of 5 reviewers — it recommended features that already exist and didn't engage the sample-size constraint. The First Principles Thinker (B) was flagged by 1 reviewer for asserting the system ignores pool competition when it doesn't.

Critical findings from review:

  • The repo's own AUDIT_DATA_LEAKAGE.md confirms in-sample calibration fitting (p_fit = p_eval = p_arr)
  • LOYO protocol documentation states SE ~0.009 per fold, making most ablation thresholds underpowered
  • The system's validation protocol acknowledges its own unreliability in comments, yet promotion gates assume it's reliable
  • Accumulated selection bias from 8 years of retrospective model development is unaddressed
