Core Dispute

Is 8.3% P(top5%) a real signal (First Principles, Expansionist) or sample artifact (Contrarian, peer reviews)?

Where the Council Clashes

Is the 8.3% P(top5%) real? The First Principles Thinker calls it a 63% lift on the metric that pays out. The Contrarian says N=17 can't support tail-metric conclusions, and the best-bracket stat may interact with bracket count differences. Peer reviews lean Contrarian: 3 of 5 flagged p=0.002 as potentially selection-biased by diversity collapse.

Mean rank vs P(top5%) as the decision metric. The Contrarian says mean rank wins over time. First Principles says P(top5%) is what pays. Both are coherent — it depends on whether you enter one bracket or many, and whether the pool pays top-5% or winner-takes-all.

Blind Spots the Council Caught

  • Leveraged mode regression is undiagnosed. Mean rank 620 vs 532 when adding stochastic tilt. This is direct evidence that adding randomness makes things worse. Every advisor arguing "fix diversity" was arguing for more randomness — but the one experiment on that produced a significant negative result. This falsifies the naive diversity hypothesis.
  • Single-bracket submission question never asked. If you submit one bracket, "12 unique brackets" is irrelevant. Diversity only matters for multi-bracket submissions.
  • The Outsider's table confusion reveals a real signal. Optimizer's BestRnk=218 vs seed's 32. In years where the baseline got lucky (top 35), the optimizer wasn't there. Is it improving the floor and ceiling, or trading one for the other?

Recommended Sequence (Gated)

  1. Diagnose leveraged mode failure. Explain why mean_rank went 532→620 with stochastic tilt. Not a hypothesis — trace it to specific bracket decisions. GATE
  2. Run held-out validation on 2023-2025. If opt_blend's P(top5%) holds out-of-sample, the signal is real. If not, stop. GATE
  3. Fix diversity collapse (hours of work). The Executor's sequencing is correct: better probs fed into a collapsed optimizer is wasted work.
  4. Wire in one data source — KenPom or Torvik, whichever has deepest historical coverage. Not all 182. Validate in isolation.
  5. Re-run backtest with both fixes applied. Now you can read the signal.

Pull the leveraged mode logs and write down, in one paragraph, exactly why mean_rank went from 532 to 620 when you added stochastic tilt. Not a hypothesis — an explanation traceable to specific bracket decisions in specific years. If you cannot write that paragraph, you do not yet understand what your optimizer is doing, and every path forward is speculation.
