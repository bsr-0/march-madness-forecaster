## Key Findings

1. **Region top-N and Exhaustive champion are the clear winners** — 8.0% and 7.7% P(1st), 2.5x seed baseline, statistically significant (p<0.001), consistent across 11/14 years. Both use raw torvik round probabilities with smart construction.

2. **The GBM learned model doesn't improve construction** — feeding GBM-predicted probabilities into region/exhaustive construction HURTS (4.6% vs 8.0%). The GBM's probabilities are less calibrated than torvik's for this purpose.

3. **Most GBM feature additions had zero effect** — multi-seed (#10), Vegas R1 (#3), and backward elimination (#12) produced identical brackets to base meta_gbm. The shallow GBM (depth=3, 50 trees) converges to the same decisions regardless.

4. **SA construction is fundamentally broken** — regardless of signal quality (chalk, volatility, fixed), SA produces terrible brackets (1-2% P(1st)). The random-flip approach doesn't work for bracket optimization.

5. **Champion picking is essentially random among 1-seeds** — the actual champion is the highest-barthag 1-seed only 2/14 times. It's the 2nd, 3rd, or even 4th-ranked 1-seed as often. No model can reliably predict which 1-seed wins OOS.

6. **Region/exhaustive win via construction quality, not champion accuracy** — 2/14 champion accuracy yet 8% P(1st). Early-round correct picks (R64-E8) accumulate enough ESPN points to win pools even with wrong champion.

7. **Train/inference context skew was a real bug** — all prior meta_gbm backtest numbers were distorted. The corrected baseline (4.6%) is higher than the old skewed number reported in CLAUDE.md (2.86%).

8. **Recency weighting strengthens the top strategies** — region/exhaustive improve from 8% → 10% P(1st) in the last 4 years, while GBM variants are flat or declining.

## Architecture Insight

The session revealed a fundamental architectural lesson: **calibrated probability sources + smart construction beats learned models + naive construction**. Torvik barthag, passed through MC simulation to get round probabilities, then optimized via region-level beam search or exhaustive champion search, outperforms a GBM trained on 15 probability bases with 39 features. The GBM adds noise to well-calibrated probabilities rather than improving them.

This suggests the research frontier is in **construction innovation** (how to build the bracket from given probabilities) rather than **probability improvement** (how to predict game outcomes). The probability bases are already near the BSS=0 ceiling; the gains come from how you assemble 63 picks into a bracket that maximizes pool-winning potential.
