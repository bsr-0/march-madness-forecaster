## Why v2 exists

**One substantive change: `TRAIN_YEARS` now extends through 2026.**

The 2026 season concluded in April 2026, months before this freeze and long
before any 2027 prediction. It is ordinary historical data for that prediction.
Excluding it would have made the system weaker than it needs to be for no
methodological reason — a real user building a 2027 bracket would obviously have
known what happened in 2026.

**The decision was made ex ante, on 2026-08-20, before any 2027 information
existed, and was not based on any 2026 performance comparison.** That ordering is
the whole point: the choice to include 2026 cannot have been influenced by a 2027
result, because no 2027 result exists.

Nothing else changed. Methodology, objectives, sampler quotas, diversity
algorithm, preference predicates, scoring, pool-size assumption and product
strategies are identical to v1.

---

## The distinction that makes this sound

Two different questions were conflated in the original framing, and separating
them is what allows v2 to be both stronger and still prospective:

| question | answer |
|---|---|
| May the model **learn** from 2026? | **Yes** — it is completed historical data available before 2027. |
| May 2026 be an **evaluation** season? | **No, permanently.** |

The contamination in `PROSPECTIVE_2027.md` is specifically:

    2026 outcomes → model development and evaluation decisions

It is *not*:

    2026 outcomes → 2027 model training

Once 2026 is treated as ordinary historical data inside an explicitly versioned
model frozen before 2027 exists, there is no scientific problem with using it.
**2026 remains barred as an evaluation holdout under v2**, exactly as under v1.

---

## Validation of the change

**The change is surgical, and this is provable rather than asserted.**
`train_noseed_model` filters training years with `y < max_year`, so extending
`TRAIN_YEARS` cannot affect any test year at or before 2026:

| test year | v1 training seasons | v2 training seasons | identical |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 2011 | 3 | 3 | yes |
| 2015 | 7 | 7 | yes |
| 2020 | 12 | 12 | yes |
| 2023 | 14 | 14 | yes |
| 2024 | 15 | 15 | yes |
| 2025 | 16 | 16 | yes |
| 2026 | 17 | 17 | yes |
| **2027** | 17 | **18** | **no — gains exactly [2026]** |

Every historical walk-forward result is therefore **unchanged by construction**,
not merely expected to be unchanged. The only prediction path that differs is
2027, which is the intended effect and the one that has not happened yet.

This also means there is no v1-vs-v2 historical comparison to run: they are
identical on every season either could be evaluated on. That is the correct
outcome, and it removes any temptation to select v2 on the basis of a
performance delta.

---

## Guardrail on how v2 was selected

v2 was **not** chosen because it beats v1 anywhere. It was chosen ex ante on the
principle:

> For the 2027 product, training includes all completed seasons through the 2026
> tournament.

If future work finds that including 2026 causes a regression, that is useful
information — but tuning v2 in response to it means leaving prospective mode and
acknowledging that the v2 freeze has been broken. Do not silently adjust.

## Everything else, unchanged from v1

The frozen coverage, the allowed/not-allowed rules, the three v1 strategies, the
required P(1st) disclosure, and the remaining known gaps are as stated in
`PROSPECTIVE_2027.md` and carried forward here without modification. The
machine-readable spec is `configs/frozen/prospective_2027_v2.json`, generated by
introspecting live code; `tests/test_frozen_2027_spec.py` fails CI on drift.

One gap from v1 is now closed: the training cutoff is 2026, not 2025. The others
remain open — stale `noseed`/`blend` baselines, no frontend yet, PBP features
outside the frozen pipeline, and the bounded seed-table provenance exception.
